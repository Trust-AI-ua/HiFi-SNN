\caption{Encoder ablation: what does the GBDT teacher contribute? Mean over 5 seeds. SOPs include the input-to-first-layer term (channels $\times$ hidden$_0$); Energy = SOPs $\times$ 0.9\,pJ. Arms: \textbf{tree} = XGBoost split edges with importance-pruned and importance-budgeted channels (ours); \textbf{quantile} = equal-frequency edges with channels identical to tree (same pruning, budget, jitter, and per-feature edge count), isolating whether the teacher's split placement matters; \textbf{no-KD} = tree encoder trained without the teacher-posterior loss terms ($\alpha_{\mathrm{kd}} = \alpha_{\mathrm{rank}} = 0$), isolating the teacher's soft labels; \textbf{uniform} = tree edges, one channel per feature with edges (changes pruning and allocation together, reported for completeness); \textbf{all} = tree edges, maximum channels per feature (dense upper bound). GBDT (teacher) is the XGBoost teacher's own test performance (not a spiking model). The teacher is trained cost-sensitively (inverse-frequency sample weights), so it operates at a balanced-performance point: on strongly imbalanced splits its raw accuracy can fall below the student's (CSE-CIC-IDS2018, UNSW-NB15) even where its macro-F1 leads, and it is a reference, not an accuracy ceiling. TTFS = mean encoder time-to-first-spike over all emitted spikes; Exit = mean confidence-triggered early-exit timestep of the trained student (both /$T{=}20$, lower = earlier).}
