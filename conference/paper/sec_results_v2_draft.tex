\subsection{The design space under the confirmation protocol}
\label{sec:res-conf}

% results/analysis/v2_full/{mean_rank.csv,friedman.json}
Under the leakage-resistant confirmation protocol, all \FNScrVariants{}
configurations were evaluated on \PH{5} protocols at five seeds, giving
\PH{25} paired (protocol $\times$ seed) blocks. \PH{Leader} attains the lowest
mean macro-F1 rank (\PH{rank}); \texttt{LeakyParallel/latency} places
\PH{position} at \PH{rank}. Friedman rejects equal performance
($\chi^2=\PH{chi}$, $p=\PH{p}$).

The Nemenyi critical difference at $k=\FNScrVariants$ and $N=\PH{25}$ is
\PH{cd}. As registered in advance, we report the post-hoc descriptively: with
this many configurations and this few blocks the all-pairs resolution is coarse,
so a failure to separate two configurations is a statement about the test's
power and not evidence that they perform equally. The comparisons that carry
weight are the pre-registered paired ones and the protocol-level collapse below.

\subsection{Which encoding leads, and where it does not}
\label{sec:res-encoding}

% results/analysis/protocol_shift/{rank_comparison.csv,encoding_aggregation.csv}
% Report block wins FIRST and the mean rank second. This ordering is deliberate
% and the paragraph states why; do not reorder it for concision.
Collapsing the \FNScrVariants{}-way ranking by encoding family, latency coding
leads: it holds rank one in \PH{k} of \PH{25} blocks, against \PH{k} for rate
and \PH{k} for delta. Its mean rank over the family is also the lowest
(\PH{rank} against \PH{rank} and \PH{rank}), but we report the block counts as
the primary summary and the mean rank as secondary, for a reason specific to
these data and given below.

The exceptions are not scattered. They fall into two classes, and each is a
protocol where the timing-isolation experiment of
Section~\ref{sec:res-timing} independently predicts that latency should not
lead. On NSL-KDD, where matched-input isolation puts the entire value of spike
timing at $\FNTimingNsl$~pp, rate coding takes \PH{k} of the five blocks. On
CTU-13 fold~1, where the same isolation makes timing a liability of
$\FNTimingCtuB$~pp, delta coding leads outright. Every block latency does not
win is a block in which timing was independently measured to be worth nothing
or worth less than nothing.

% This convergence is the point of the subsection; it is what makes the
% exceptions evidence for the account rather than against it.
The NSL-KDD case is worth stating separately, because three measurements of
different kinds agree on it. Rate leads latency there under the screening
protocol (\PH{rank} against \PH{rank}) and again under the confirmation
protocol (\PH{rank} against \PH{rank}), a margin that survives a change of
protocol designed to remove the optimism of the first. The matched-input
isolation, which shares no analysis path with either ranking, puts the value of
timing on that protocol at $\FNTimingNsl$~pp. A rank aggregation over
\FNScrVariants{} configurations and a single-factor ablation at identical input
activity are not the same instrument, and they return the same verdict:
on NSL-KDD, temporal coding buys nothing.

Finally, the reason for preferring block counts. The encoding means are
compressed by a single protocol: on CTU-13 fold~1 the leading family sits at
\PH{rank} while latency sits at \PH{rank}, a displacement of \PH{k} rank
positions concentrated in one condition out of \PH{5}. Averaging that into a
family mean produces a number describing neither the typical protocol nor the
exception, in the same way that a standard deviation pooled across datasets of
different difficulty describes neither (Section~\ref{sec:res-strict}). The
block counts are not subject to that: a protocol where the ordering inverts
contributes its five blocks and no more, whatever the size of the inversion.
Where the two summaries disagree in emphasis, the disaggregated one is the one
we rely on.

\label{sec:res-shift}

This is the question the study exists to answer, and it reduces to one number.
The same \FNScrVariants{} configurations ranked under the two protocols give a
Kendall rank correlation of $\tau=\PH{tau}$ (permutation $p=\PH{taup}$,

Figure~\ref{fig:rankshift} shows the movement configuration by configuration.

% results/v1_submitted/ctu13_strict_vanilla.csv
The displacement is not uniform across datasets, and CTU-13 shows where it comes
from. Under the screening protocol, with whole-scenario host aggregates and a
random flow split, \texttt{LeakyParallel/latency} attains \FNCtuLegacy{}
macro-F1. Making the aggregation causal alone leaves \FNCtuCausalRandom.
Adding a scenario-disjoint partition brings it to $\mathbf{\FNCtuStrict\%}$,
with a standard deviation of \FNCtuSdEval{} percentage points over the
\FNCtuNEval{} fold-seed evaluations and fold means, in fold order, of
\FNCtuIdxFA, \FNCtuIdxFB, \FNCtuIdxFC{} and \FNCtuIdxFD. A single benchmark
therefore accounts for much of the ranking movement, and it does so because the
screening partition let scenario- and host-related information cross the
evaluation boundary.
