\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{No non-spiking baselines.}
This study compares spiking configurations against one another and includes no
decision-tree ensemble, linear model or architecture-matched dense network. It
therefore cannot support any claim that spiking detectors are more accurate or
more efficient than conventional machine learning, and we make none. Readers
should treat every efficiency figure here as a comparison \emph{among} spiking
configurations. A like-for-like comparison against conventional models under
identical preprocessing is a natural companion study and remains open.

\paragraph{Confirmation is targeted, not exhaustive.}
A complete leakage-resistant re-run of all 27 configurations costs approximately
$198$ GPU-hours on our hardware and was beyond the revision budget. We therefore
report the full 27-configuration sweep as a \emph{screening} experiment under
the original protocol, and confirm the selected configuration under the strict
protocol along the two design axes that pass through it. The strict evidence for
configurations far from the selected one is consequently thinner than for the
selected one itself, and a ranking reversal somewhere in the middle of the field
under the strict protocol would not be visible to us.

\paragraph{Neuron thresholds are not exhaustively tuned.}
\texttt{SLSTM} and \texttt{SConv2dLSTM} use a firing threshold of $0.1$ while the
other seven families use $1.0$, because at $1.0$ the gated cells emit no spikes
on sparse tabular input and collapse to the majority class. The sweep is
therefore not a pure neuron-only ablation: part of the difference between the
gated families and the rest is attributable to the threshold rather than to the
cell dynamics. We did not run the full threshold grid --- at four thresholds
across nine families it approaches $170$ GPU-hours --- so we cannot rule out
that a better-chosen threshold would move a family's position in the ranking.
What we can say is that the selected configuration is not a gated family, so its
rank does not depend on that choice.

\paragraph{CIC-IDS2017 ordering is a proxy.}
The public redistribution we use retains the flow features and label but not the
original \texttt{Timestamp} field. Within a capture our partition therefore
orders by row position, which is the flow-writer's output order and
chronological only up to reordering at flow completion. We describe this
protocol as order-disjoint rather than temporal for that reason. The day-level
grouping used for the transfer experiment is exact, as it derives from the
capture file names rather than from row content.

\paragraph{CTU-13 is scenario-disjoint, not host-disjoint.}
Whole scenarios are held out and no scenario is split across the partition
boundary, but source hosts recur across scenarios: $76.2\%$ of the fold-1 test
hosts also appear in training. The protocol therefore removes scenario-level
leakage and the feature-level leak from non-causal aggregation, but it does not
establish generalisation to unseen hosts. Its fold-to-fold spread is large and
we report the range rather than the mean alone, because a mean across folds
whose difficulty differs this much conceals more than it summarises.

\paragraph{Benchmark duplication.}
$57.53\%$ of the official KDDCup99 test set occurs verbatim in the training
split, and $2.68\%$ of NSL-KDD's. We report duplicate-free sensitivity figures
alongside the headline numbers rather than removing the duplicates and thereby
departing from the canonical evaluation. On KDDCup99 the effect is small
($-0.79$ pp macro-F1), but the magnitude of the overlap means absolute
KDDCup99 scores should not be read as evidence of generalisation.

\paragraph{Rare classes and unseen attack types.}
We train on the natural class distribution without resampling. The R2L and U2R
classes are severely under-represented and their per-class F1 is correspondingly
low; we report per-class support alongside every per-class metric so that a
score computed on tens of samples is not read as comparable to one computed on
hundreds of thousands. Separately, $29.2\%$ of NSL-KDD's official test attacks
belong to types absent from training, and detection on those types is far weaker
than on known types. This gap is a property of the detection problem rather than
of any one configuration, but it bounds what our headline detection rates mean.

\paragraph{Operating thresholds transfer imperfectly.}
Fixed false-alarm operating points are selected on validation data and frozen
before evaluation, as they must be. Calibration itself is sound --- realised
validation false-alarm rates land on target --- but transfer to test varies by
protocol, from essentially exact on CIC-IDS2017 to a large discrepancy on the
hardest CTU-13 fold. We report realised test rates beside their targets rather
than the targets alone. This is a statement about threshold transfer under
distribution shift, not about discrimination: a detector may rank samples well
while a threshold calibrated elsewhere ceases to mean what it was set to mean.

\paragraph{CIC-IDS2017 has no U2R test support.}
The order-disjoint partition places no U2R samples in the held-out interval,
because the mapped U2R events occur earlier in the capture sequence. We did not
alter the frozen split to correct this: changing a partition after observing its
class composition would cost more in pre-registration credibility than the
adjustment is worth. The consequence is that the fixed-universe macro-F1 on this
protocol is capped at \FNCicCeiling{} and its absolute value is not comparable
across datasets, though within-protocol comparisons and all rank-based
statistics are unaffected because the penalty is identical for every model.

\paragraph{Energy figures are proxies.}
We report synaptic operation counts, which are hardware-independent, and where
we convert them to energy we do so with a published per-operation constant and
label the result an operation-based proxy. These are not measurements on
neuromorphic hardware, and they do not account for memory traffic, which on real
deployments is frequently the dominant cost. Wall-clock inference times were
measured on a shared GPU and are comparable across our variants but should not
be read as absolute deployment latencies.

\paragraph{Delta encoding is evaluated outside its intended regime.}
Delta modulation encodes temporal contrast, whereas our inputs are static
per-flow feature vectors presented independently. Its weaker aggregate
performance here is therefore at least partly a representational mismatch and
should not be read as evidence that delta modulation is inherently unsuited to
intrusion detection. Our own results make that caution concrete rather than
rhetorical: on CTU-13 fold~1 delta achieves the highest terminal macro-F1 of the
three encodings, exceeding latency by $11.2$ percentage points. Delta is
therefore generally weaker under the evaluated static-flow representation but
demonstrably capable of winning under a sufficiently difficult protocol, which
also answers the concern that the encoding ranking might exist only because
delta was configured to fail. We accordingly base the encoding conclusion on the
latency-versus-rate comparison and treat delta as a secondary observation about
this formulation.

\paragraph{Fixed capacity.}
Architecture ($T=25$, $H=128$) and training budget are held constant across all
configurations to keep the comparison clean. Absolute scores would shift under
larger networks or longer training. $T$ in particular is consequential for a
timing-based code, and we did not sweep it; our anytime-readout analysis
characterises behaviour \emph{within} the fixed window rather than across
alternative window lengths.
